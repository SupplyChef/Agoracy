# Public accountability scorecards: methodology

**Draft 1, 27 Oct 2026.** Applies to every Agoracy scorecard. Florida's Legislature is the pilot; the U.S. Congress is next. Read [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) first: its sourcing rules apply here in full.

## What a scorecard is, and what it is not

A scorecard records what elected officials did in office, links each item to the public record, and explains why it matters. It is written for the same reader as the posts: a skeptical voter or legislator who distrusts advocacy from either side and wants to check the facts.

It is not a test of who may hold office. It does not grade anyone's religion, party, ideology or policy preferences. A legislator is never scored for being religious, secular, conservative or liberal, or for opposing a particular policy. The question in every entry is narrower: did this official give honest, evidence-responsive reasons for using public power, respect the equal rights of people who don't share their beliefs, and handle public trust responsibly?

## Who is scored

- **Everyone in the chamber, under the same rules.** No starting list by party. Each sitting member gets a row in the roster, even when the row is empty.
- **Conduct in office within the window.** For Florida, the window is the 2023 session through the most recent session. Older conduct can go in the notes for context but is not scored.
- **Former members and officials in other branches** go to their own file. Executive-branch actors (for example, in the Hope Florida case) belong in a statewide-officials file, not the legislative ranking. Members who moved to Congress carry their record to the congressional scorecard.

## Six categories

The weights below are the ones agreed with RJ. They set priorities; they do not claim that integrity can be measured to a decimal.

| Category | Weight | What counts as evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Integrity and corruption risks | 25 percent | Self-dealing, conflicts of interest, gifts, misuse of office, ethics and court findings, handling of public money |
| Scientific integrity | 15 percent | Factual claims about science made to justify official action; whether the official cited, ignored or misstated the evidence; corrections |
| Secular government | 15 percent | Government endorsement of religion; equal treatment of every belief and of nonbelief |
| Intellectual honesty | 15 percent | Verifiably false or misleading public statements; willingness to correct them |
| Constitutional principles and equal citizenship | 15 percent | Civil liberties, equal protection, democratic procedures, treatment of groups of citizens |
| Transparency and accountability | 15 percent | Disclosures, public records, oversight of government, cooperation with investigations |

## Evidence status: every entry gets exactly one

1. **Formal finding.** A court judgment, criminal conviction, ethics commission final order or probable-cause finding, legislative ethics report, auditor or inspector general finding. Described exactly as the body worded it, at the stage it reached. A probable-cause finding is not a final violation; an ethics finding is not a crime.
2. **Established fact.** Shown by an official record: a bill's text and history, a roll call, a chaptered law, an official transcript or video, a financial disclosure, a campaign-finance filing, a sworn statement.
3. **Unresolved concern.** Conduct that warrants scrutiny but rests on allegations, reporting, or a record that is incomplete or contested. Published with that label. **Not scored.** It can move to one of the other two statuses when the record catches up.

Three things never count as evidence of misconduct: an allegation alone; a complaint dismissed for "lack of legal sufficiency" (the Florida Commission on Ethics reviews such complaints on their face, without investigating them); and a pending criminal charge. A finding of "no probable cause" is recorded in the official's favor.

## Sources

Use the hierarchy in CLAUDE.md: enacted law and chapter numbers, bill text and staff analyses, journals and roll calls, official video, court opinions, ethics commission orders, auditor reports, campaign-finance and disclosure filings, and agency data. News reports point to records and supply on-the-record quotes; they are not enough by themselves. Advocacy groups on any side are cited only for what they said.

**Scientific benchmarks.** Where an entry turns on science, cite the strongest available assessment of the evidence: the National Academies, systematic reviews, and the statements of the relevant professional bodies. Where federal agencies have recently changed position, say so and cite both. The test is not whether a legislator reached a different policy conclusion from scientists. It is whether the legislator misstated what the evidence shows.

## What an entry must contain

1. The official, chamber, district and party (party for identification only).
2. Their role: prime sponsor, co-sponsor, committee vote, floor vote, statement, or subject of a finding.
3. The exact action or words, with a link to the record and the date.
4. Its evidence status (above) and the stage of any proceeding.
5. What the record establishes, and what it does not.
6. The strongest counterargument, from a named, on-the-record voice where one exists.
7. The official's response, or the date we asked and got none.
8. The proposed score and the reason, or "not scored" and the reason.

## Scoring rules (proposed; to settle with RJ before any ranking)

**Entry score.** Each scored entry gets a value from -3 to +3 in one category:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| -3 | Formal finding of serious misconduct, or a verifiably false factual claim used to justify enacted law and not corrected |
| -2 | Clear, documented conduct against the category's standard, with real effect |
| -1 | Documented conduct against the standard with limited effect, or later corrected or apologized for |
| 0 | Recorded for context; not scored (policy disagreement, unresolved concern, dismissed complaint) |
| +1 | Documented conduct that meets the standard at some cost, such as an evidence-based argument on the record against one's own party |
| +2 | Sustained, documented work for the standard, such as leading oversight of one's own side |
| +3 | Exceptional, such as correcting one's own public error on the record, or exposing misconduct with documented results |

**Role multiplier.** Prime sponsor or author of the statement: 1.0. Co-sponsor: 0.5. Committee or floor vote alone: 0.25.

**Outcome multiplier** for legislation. Enacted: 1.0. Passed one chamber: 0.75. Died in committee: 0.5.

**Votes alone rarely decide anything.** Most contested Florida bills pass on near-party-line votes. A ranking built mainly on roll calls would rank the parties, not the people, which is what this project promises not to do. So a vote is scored only when the member also spoke or acted on the record, or when the vote broke with their party and they explained why.

**Positive evidence is required for the top list.** The absence of bad conduct is not a record. The top list needs documented positive entries (+1 or better) in at least two categories.

**Threshold to be ranked at all.** A member needs at least three scored entries, in at least two categories, before they can appear on either list. If fewer than ten members meet the threshold for a list, the list is published shorter, with a note. We do not pad a list.

**Category totals.** Sum entry scores within each category, cap each category at ±6 so that no single issue swamps the rest, convert to the category weight, and publish all six category scores beside the total. The total is never published without them.

## Right of response and corrections

- Before publication, each ranked member's office gets the full text of their entries by email, with 14 days to respond.
- Responses are published verbatim (or linked, if long) beside the entry.
- A factual error is corrected in place, with a dated line in the change log. A disputed interpretation stays, with the dispute shown.
- Any member can ask for a review of their entries at any time.

## Timing and independence

- No ranking is published in the 30 days before an election in which the ranked members are on the ballot. Evidence files can be.
- The authors disclose their own affiliations and donations on the methodology page.
- Every list is reproducible: the evidence register and the scoring sheet are published with it.
