## FL-001. School religious-expression amendment (HJR 583, 2026)

**Record.** HJR 583, "Protection of Religious Expression in Public Schools," by Reps. Chase Tramont (R, District 30) and Berny Jacques (R, District 59). Co-introducers: Reps. Bankson, Boyles, Daniels, Fabricio, Holcomb, Michael, Nix, Partington, Plakon, Plasencia, Rizo and Salzman. Rep. Kimberly Daniels is a Democrat; the others are Republicans. The House passed it 93-17 on 4 Feb 2026. It died in the Senate Rules Committee on 13 Mar 2026, as did its identical Senate companion, SJR 1104 by Sen. Ralph Massullo (R). As a joint resolution it would have gone to voters, needing 60 percent. [Senate bill page]

**What it would have done** (as summarized by WLRN/Florida Phoenix, to check against the bill text): protect students' religious expression in schoolwork, dress, prayer and clubs on the same terms as secular expression; let staff join student-initiated religious activity outside the school day; require a moment of silence in first period; and let high schools at championship contests request up to two minutes of opening remarks over the public address system, which may include student-led or staff-led prayer.

**Both sides, on the record.**
- Rep. Chad Johnson (R), who voted yes: "The right to religious freedom is a God-given right that deserves the utmost protection." He argued that statutes can be changed and a constitutional protection is more secure.
- Rep. Anna Eskamani (D), who voted no, said it "opens the door to confusion, litigation and the erosion of the separation between church and state."
- University of Florida law professor Timothy McLendon: "Legally, it's hard to see how it adds anything to what a student is already able to do."
- University of Florida law professor Jane Bambauer, on the championship-contest provision: "That part of the law raises my eyebrows." She said staff remarks over a school PA system may become an official religious act.

**What this establishes.** The sponsors sought to put religious-expression protections into the constitution. Most of those protections restate rights students already have.

**What it does not establish.** Sponsorship alone does not show intent to favor one religion. The one provision with a live church-state question is staff-led prayer over the PA system at championship games.

**Proposed scoring.** Not scored until a legal analysis compares that provision with *Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe* (2000), which struck down student-led prayer over a school PA system at football games, and *Kennedy v. Bremerton School District* (2022). If the analysis finds the provision invites the practice *Santa Fe* struck down, score -2 for the prime sponsors under secular government, times 0.75 (passed one chamber).

**Still to collect.** The bill text and House staff analysis; Rep. Tramont's and Rep. Jacques's committee and floor remarks (Florida Channel video); the roll call; responses from both offices.

## FL-002. Geoengineering and weather-modification ban (SB 56, 2025)

**Record.** CS/CS/SB 56 by Sen. Ileana Garcia (R, District 36); co-introducers Sens. Leek, Yarborough and Gruters. House companion CS/CS/HB 477 by the State Affairs Committee, the Natural Resources and Disasters Subcommittee, and Reps. Steele and Mayfield; co-introducers Benarroch, Borrero, Miller and Nix. The Senate passed SB 56 28-9 on 3 Apr 2025; the House passed it 82-28 on 30 Apr 2025. Approved by the Governor 20 Jun 2025; chapter 2025-157, effective 1 Jul 2025.

**What the law does** (enrolled text). It bans releasing any substance into the atmosphere "for the express purpose of affecting the temperature, weather, climate, or intensity of sunlight"; makes violations by aircraft operators a third-degree felony; raises civil fines to up to $100,000; tells the Department of Environmental Protection to take and screen public reports of suspected activity; requires public-use airports to report monthly to the Department of Transportation; and repeals the state's weather-modification licensing law.

**Sponsor's words** (Florida Phoenix, Senate committee, 18 Mar 2025): "Many of us senators receive concerns, complaints, on a regular basis regarding these condensation trails." "There is a lot of skepticism in regard to this." The bill would "try to separate fact from fiction" by "creating a system to log, track, investigate if necessary." On the Senate floor (Florida Phoenix, 3 Apr 2025): "Don't you have a problem with people spraying things into the atmosphere that really have no type of empirical data?"

**The other side.** Sen. Tina Polsky (D) asked on the floor how the department could investigate a trail that has already dissipated. Augustus Doricko of Rainmaker Technology, a cloud-seeding company, opposed the bill as drafted, saying it would hinder work on water scarcity. In committee, Democratic Sens. Lori Berman and Kristen Arrington voted no; Democratic Sens. Jason Pizzo and Barbara Sharief voted yes.

**Scientific benchmark.** Weather modification such as cloud seeding is real and was licensed under the law this bill repealed. Claims of a secret large-scale spraying program ("chemtrails") are not supported: in a 2016 survey of 77 atmospheric chemists and geochemists (Shearer and others, *Environmental Research Letters*), 76 said they had found no evidence of one. [Link to add.]

**What this establishes.** The Legislature banned deliberate weather modification and built a reporting system for suspected activity, in bipartisan votes.

**What it does not establish.** None of the sponsor's quoted words asserts that chemtrails exist; she framed the bill as a way to test constituents' complaints. Banning a real but small industry is a policy choice, not a scientific error.

**Proposed scoring.** Pending the full committee and floor video. Score negative under scientific integrity only if the sponsor stated as fact something the evidence contradicts.

**Cut from the pasted draft.** "Removed state authority to conduct certain weather-modification research." Not confirmed: the enrolled bill amends s. 373.026(6), F.S., only as a conforming change, and that subsection still directs DEP to run "a program of study, research, and experimentation and evaluation in the field of weather modification." Also cut: "Kevin Steele" as House sponsor by full name. The House page lists only "Steele"; no Rep. Steele is on today's House roster, so identify him from the 2025 member list before using the name.

## FL-003. Ban on government net-zero policies (HB 1217, 2026)

**Record.** HB 1217 by Reps. John Snyder (R, District 86) and Berny Jacques (R, District 59); co-sponsor Rep. Chase Tramont (R); committee substitute by the Commerce Committee. Committee votes 12-3, 11-4 and 19-4. The House passed it 80-29 on 5 Mar 2026; the Senate substituted it for SB 7046 and passed it 24-12 on 11 Mar 2026. Approved by the Governor on 22 Apr 2026 (governor's press release); chapter 2026-45, effective 1 Jul 2026. Senate companion SB 1628 by Sen. Bryan Avila (R).

**Source caution.** The Senate's bill page, when fetched on 27 Oct 2026, showed only the bill's January history. The history and votes above come from LobbyTools and FastDemocracy, which agree with each other and with the governor's release. Confirm against the House Journal before publication.

**What the law does** (governor's release): bars state and local governments from adopting or enforcing net-zero emissions policies, including carbon taxes and cap-and-trade, bars public money for groups promoting such policies, and requires annual compliance reports.

**Both sides.** Rep. Snyder argued for reliable energy from "diversified sources" (Florida Phoenix). Rep. Jacques: "I proudly sponsored HB 1217 to fight for jobs and affordability in Florida." Rep. Lindsay Cross (D): "Local governments are on the front lines of our climate crisis." "The least we can do is get out of the way and let our locals lead."

**Proposed scoring.** 0. No coverage found shows either sponsor misstating climate science; their stated reasons are cost and reliability. Opposing net-zero mandates is a policy position. Reopen only if the committee video shows a false factual claim.

## FL-004. Statewide fluoride ban (SB 700, 2025)

**Record.** CS/CS/CS/SB 700 (Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services) by the Agriculture Committee and Sen. Keith Truenow (R, District 13). The Senate passed it 27-9 on 16 Apr 2025; the House 88-27 on 29 Apr 2025. Approved 15 May 2025; chapter 2025-22. Among other things it bars adding fluoride to public water. The Senate vote page also lists a 13-21 tally dated 15 Apr 2025 that does not match the bill history; check the Senate Journal.

**Both sides.** Sen. Truenow: "We're here to hydrate, not medicate." Sen. Tina Polsky (D): "This has been proven science for the last 50 years," calling attacks on fluoridation the "conspiracy theory du jour." Sen. Barbara Sharief (D), a nurse practitioner, urged senators to "stand with science." Florida Surgeon General Joseph Ladapo has argued fluoridation carries health risks. (Quotes from WUSF, 17 Apr 2025.)

**Scientific benchmark.** The U.S. Public Health Service recommends 0.7 mg/L. The National Toxicology Program's 2024 review found, with moderate confidence, lower IQ in children at exposures above 1.5 mg/L, about twice that level, and said the evidence at 0.7 mg/L was insufficient to judge. [Links to add.] So the evidence supports community fluoridation's dental benefit but leaves a real question at the margin.

**Proposed scoring.** Pending. Because the evidence is not one-sided, the ban itself is not scored as a science failure. Score only specific claims, by the sponsor or any member, that misstate the NTP or dental evidence.

## FL-005. Vaccine "conscience" exemption (SB 1756, 2026)

**Record.** SB 1756, "Medical Freedom," by Sen. Clay Yarborough (R, District 4). It kept school vaccine requirements but added a "conscience" exemption, required vaccinators to offer an alternative schedule, and let pharmacists sell ivermectin without a prescription. Senate Appropriations passed it 10-7 on 24 Feb 2026, with Republican Sens. Gayle Harrell and Tom Wright voting no; Republican Sens. Colleen Burton and Ana Maria Rodriguez had voted no in earlier committees. The Senate passed it on 9 Mar 2026 (Capitolist; count to confirm). The House companion, HB 917 by Rep. Jeff Holcomb (R), was never heard. Speaker Daniel Perez: "A bill that hasn't moved in the House is not going to be brought up at this time." A special session from 28 Apr 2026 took up "medical freedom" again; outcome to confirm.

**Both sides.** Sen. Yarborough: "Parents have a fundamental right to choose what they believe is best for their children." Senate President Ben Albritton: "The default decision-maker in a child's life is the parent – not the government." Sen. Harrell: "I truly believe that this is a dangerous bill, and I cannot vote for it," and "I think vaccination policy should not be about politics."

**Proposed scoring.** Pending the floor transcript and a public-health benchmark on how non-medical exemptions affect outbreak risk. Sen. Harrell is a candidate for +1 (scientific integrity): an evidence-based argument on the record against her own party's priority bill.

**Not legislative.** The Department of Health's proposed rule (approved by Dr. Ladapo on 20 Aug 2026) to drop four vaccines from school entry requirements is an agency action and goes to the statewide-officials file.

## FL-006. The Hope Florida inquiry (2025)

**What the court record establishes.** In *In re Leon County Grand Jury Presentment, Fall of 2025*, No. 1D2026-2435 (Fla. 1st DCA, 7 Oct 2026), Judge Lance Neff, for the court, recounted that in September 2024 the state resolved Medicaid overcharge allegations against Centene Corp. for $67,048,611: $57,048,611 to the state and $10 million to the Hope Florida Foundation, a direct-support organization. The foundation approved two $5 million grants to Secure Florida's Future and Save Our Society From Drugs, both 501(c)(4) groups, which donated to Keep Florida Clean, a political committee opposing the 2024 marijuana amendment. A Leon County grand jury returned no indictment and on 28 Jan 2026 issued a presentment calling the $10 million "misappropriated." The court ordered the presentment expunged in full: the grand jury had condemned named people without indicting them, and the state attorney had sent it to at least 49 recipients before those people could challenge it. The court said: "The $10 million provided to Hope Florida in the Centene settlement was never public money," and noted that the Legislature has not made such third-party payments illegal. Judge M.K. Thomas concurred; Judge Bilbrey concurred in part, on the disclosure ground only.

**The legislator's role.** Rep. Alex Andrade (R, District 2) chaired the House Health Care Budget Subcommittee, which held hearings on the money in April 2025. The final hearing on 24 Apr 2025 ended after invited witnesses declined to appear; the House did not subpoena them. Andrade said Attorney General James Uthmeier, who chaired Keep Florida Clean while the governor's chief of staff, "transferred this money illicitly, misled people and lied to people in the process." AHCA Secretary Shevaun Harris said the $10 million was not taxpayer money. (Florida Phoenix, 24 Apr 2025.)

**The response to him.** In February 2026 Mr. Uthmeier wrote that "Someone aligned with Planned Parenthood should not be chairing a subcommittee focused on the health of Floridians," because Andrade's law firm represents Planned Parenthood in a state lawsuit, and said his office would not cooperate with the subcommittee. Andrade: "I don't represent Planned Parenthood & I'm a salaried employee of the law firm I work for."

**Proposed scoring.** Candidate +2 for Andrade under transparency and accountability: he led oversight of his own party's administration. Caveat: his accusation that the money moved "illicitly" went further than the 1st DCA later found, so the intellectual-honesty category should record that the court rejected the legal premise, and invite his response.

**Not in the legislative ranking.** The settlement's executive-branch actors (Gov. Ron DeSantis's office, AHCA, then-Attorney General Ashley Moody, Mr. Uthmeier). Their file needs the settlement drafts, foundation minutes, grant records and Keep Florida Clean's campaign-finance reports.

**Cut or held from the pasted draft.** The "$8.5 million" that reached Keep Florida Clean: not in the court opinion; verify from the committee's filings with the Division of Elections. PolitiFact's 22 Oct 2026 piece and the Florida Trident's email reporting: not yet read; to check. Florida Trident reports that the officials' 16 Oct motion to bar publication of the expunged report was denied the same day; that litigation is ongoing.
