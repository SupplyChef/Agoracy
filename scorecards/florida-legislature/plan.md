# Florida Legislature accountability scorecard: plan and status

**Draft 1, 27 Oct 2026.** Pilot for the [scorecard methodology](../methodology.md). Evidence so far is in the [evidence register](evidence-register.md); every seat is in [roster.csv](roster.csv).

## Goal

RJ's goal: publish the 10 Florida legislators with the strongest records of responsible representation and the 10 whose documented conduct is most concerning, scored on conduct, not religion or party, under the same rules for both parties. Then apply the method to Florida's delegation in Congress.

## Where it stands

**Can we name a top 10 and a bottom 10 today? No.** Of 155 sitting members (five seats are vacant), 46 have at least one entry in the register, and none yet has a scored entry. Under the methodology's threshold (three scored entries in two categories), nobody qualifies for either list. Publishing names now would rest on one bill or one quote per person, which is the kind of list a skeptical reader can dismiss in a sentence.

What the first pass does show:

- **The pasted cases mostly check out, but they are thin on individual conduct.** Bill sponsorships, votes and outcomes for HJR 583, SB 56 and HB 1217 are confirmed. What would turn them into scores, the sponsors' own factual claims, is in committee and floor video we have not yet reviewed. Coverage of HB 1217 and SB 56 shows no sponsor asserting a false scientific claim.
- **Two pasted claims failed or are unconfirmed.** SB 56 did not clearly remove the state's weather-modification research authority, and the $8.5 million figure in the Hope Florida chain is not in the court record.
- **Hope Florida is mainly an executive-branch case.** The only sitting legislator with a central role is Rep. Alex Andrade, who led the House inquiry. The officials at the center of the settlement belong in a separate statewide-officials file.
- **Formal findings against sitting members are rare.** The Ethics Commission releases read so far (11 months of 2025-26) show no adverse finding against a sitting legislator: two no-probable-cause findings for Rep. Basabe and six complaints dismissed on their face.
- **Positive evidence exists and should be collected on purpose.** Rep. Andrade's oversight of his own party's administration and Sen. Gayle Harrell's on-the-record opposition to the vaccine-exemption bill are examples of what the top list should be built from.
- **Most entries so far involve Republicans,** because Republicans hold about 70 percent of seats and wrote nearly all the bills the pasted draft chose. That is a selection effect we have to correct by sweeping every member, not by adding Democratic cases to balance the count.

## Research plan to make the lists defensible

1. **Bill sweep (2023-2026 sessions).** Pull every bill in the six categories' subject areas: religion in schools and government; public health, vaccines, fluoride, medical practice; climate, energy and environment; elections and voting; public records and ethics; civil liberties. For each: sponsor, co-sponsors, text, staff analysis, outcome and roll calls. Source: the Legislature's bill pages and journals.
2. **Statements.** For each sponsored bill, review the committee and floor video (Florida Channel) and log every checkable factual claim, verbatim, with the timestamp. This is the step that turns sponsorship into a score.
3. **Integrity records.** Read every Ethics Commission release from Jan 2023 on (fill the months missing above); pull each member's Form 6 financial disclosure; search campaign-finance filings for gifts and related-party payments; check court dockets and inspector-general and Auditor General reports that name members.
4. **Positive record.** Log oversight hearings members led, bills they sponsored on transparency or ethics, public corrections of their own errors, and evidence-based breaks with their party, all with records.
5. **Right of response.** Send every member with a scored entry their entries; wait 14 days; publish responses.
6. **Score, then review.** Score with the rules in the methodology, publish all six category scores, and have one reviewer from each side read the lists before publication, per CLAUDE.md's review step.

A realistic first milestone is steps 1 and 3 for the 2026 session alone, which would give every member some record and show whether the threshold is set at a level enough members can reach.

## Timing

The general election is on 3 Nov 2026, a week from this draft, and every House seat and half the Senate are on the ballot. The methodology bars publishing rankings within 30 days of an election in which the ranked members run. So the first ranking should come after the election, against the new Legislature's roster, using the 2023-2026 record of those who return. Evidence files can be published sooner.

## Then Congress

Florida's 28 House members and two senators, using the same methodology. Benchmarks already in the register: former Rep. Matt Gaetz (R), former Rep. Sheila Cherfilus-McCormick (D) and Rep. Randy Fine (R). Primary sources: House and Senate Ethics Committee reports, Office of Congressional Conduct referrals, court records, roll calls from clerk.house.gov and senate.gov, and FEC filings.
